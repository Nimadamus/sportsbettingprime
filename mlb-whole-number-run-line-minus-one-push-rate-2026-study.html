The Push Is The Product: 27.27 Percent Of 2026 MLB Games Are One Run Games

September 6, 2026 | MLB | Whole Number Run Line | Sports Betting Prime

Everybody knows the run line as -1.5. Lay a run and a half, get plus money, lose the ticket when your team wins 4 to 3. It is the standard baseball spread and it is the reason so many people who pick the right side still lose.

There is a second version of that bet, offered as -1, and almost nobody talks about what it actually costs and what it actually buys. So I pulled every completed 2026 regular season game, 2,138 of them, and measured the thing the whole number line is built around.

Not a common result. The most common result, by a wide margin, and it is not close.

More than one in four games in 2026 has landed on the exact number that separates a -1 ticket from a -1.5 ticket. On -1.5 those 583 games were losses. On -1 they were refunds. That is the entire product, and it is worth pricing properly.

What A -1 Ticket Really Looks Like

Season win percentage is a rough proxy for how often a club is favoured, so split all 4,276 team games by the club's own record and look at the three outcomes that matter: win by two or more, win by exactly one, lose.

For a genuine contender, laying -1 has cashed 50.77 percent of decided tickets this season against 42.77 percent for -1.5. That is eight full percentage points, and it comes entirely from the 583 one run games moving out of the loss column.

A .550 team at -1 breaks even at roughly -103. The same team at -1.5 needs +134 to break even. If a book is charging you -130 for -1 and paying +120 for -1.5, it is charging more than the push is worth. If it is charging -105 to -135 for -1, that is the fair band.
Home Favourites Push The Most

Split the good teams by venue and the push rate is not stable. It is a home effect, and it is a large one.

.550 or better teamTeam gamesWin by 2+Push on -1Lose
At home49841.57%19.08%39.36%
On the road49843.98%12.45%43.57%

Nearly one in five home games for a contender ends in a one run win. The reason is structural rather than mysterious. The home team stops batting the moment it takes the lead in the ninth, so a walkoff single that would have been a three run inning on the road gets recorded as a one run win. A good part of the extra push rate at home is games that simply ended early.

That flips the usual instinct. Most people lay the bigger number at home because the home side feels safer. The 2026 data says the home contender is the one whose margin gets clipped, and the whole number line is where you want to be when it happens.

If you are laying -1 with the Reds you are buying a lot of refund. If you are laying -1 with the Astros you are paying for a push that mostly does not arrive, and you would rather have the -1.5 and the plus money.

How To Use It Today

Seattle at home against Oakland is the clean live example. The Mariners win by exactly one run in 31.8 percent of their wins, they are a home side that stops hitting when it goes ahead in the ninth, and Oakland is the worst run prevention club on today's board at 5.71 allowed a game. The -1.5 is a ticket you lose on every walkoff. The -1 turns those into refunds.

The whole number line was quoted at -145 in one shop and -133 in another this morning, on the same game, at the same minute. That gap is bigger than the edge in most bets people spend all week hunting for. Price the push, then shop it.

Three rules fall out of the data and none of them require a model.

One. If you are on a contender, the whole number line is worth roughly eight points of win rate over -1.5, so it is worth paying up to about -103 before it stops being the better ticket.

Two. Home contenders push far more than road contenders, 19.08 percent against 12.45. Take -1 at home and take -1.5 on the road, not the other way round.

Three. Club identity matters more than the league average. Some rosters produce one run wins as a habit and some almost never do, and that is the difference between a refund you paid for and a refund you never see.
