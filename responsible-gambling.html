Self-exclusion

Every licensed sportsbook is required to offer deposit limits, wager limits, time-outs and self-exclusion in account settings. They take effect immediately and cannot be reversed by customer service on request, which is the point.

Most US states also run a statewide self-exclusion list covering every licensed operator at once.

The maths, stated plainly

A standard -110 price requires you to win 52.38% of your bets simply to break even. The gap between that and 50% is the sportsbook's margin, and it is charged on every bet you place, win or lose.

Over a long enough run, the overwhelming majority of bettors lose money. This is not a failure of discipline or research on their part; it is what the pricing is designed to produce. Understanding the market well changes the size and shape of that loss. It does not convert betting into income.

Warning signs

  • Betting more than you planned, or more often than you planned
  • Chasing losses with larger stakes to get back to even
  • Betting with money set aside for something else
  • Hiding the extent of your betting from people close to you
  • Feeling that you need to bet to feel normal, or that a win will fix something
  • Borrowing to bet, in any form, including a credit card

Any one of these is worth taking seriously. Several together is not a phase.

Practical limits that actually help

  • Fund a separate account and never top it up mid-session
  • Set a deposit limit before you need one, while the decision is easy
  • Fix a unit size as a percentage of bankroll and do not move it after a loss
  • Keep a record of every bet — the honest number is usually worse than the remembered one
  • Take a scheduled break, not one triggered by a bad week
