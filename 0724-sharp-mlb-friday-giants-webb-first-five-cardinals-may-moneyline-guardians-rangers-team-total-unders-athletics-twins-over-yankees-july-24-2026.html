Six Bets On The Arms, One Bet Against Them

July 24, 2026 | MLB Moneyline, First-Five, Team Total and Game Total Reads | Sports Betting Prime

The biggest number on tonight's card asks you to buy exactly five innings. Not a win, not nine innings, not a save from a Giants bullpen carrying a 4.40 staff ERA. Five innings of Logan Webb at minus 175, and then the ticket is graded and you are free to stop watching. That is the shape of the whole night. Six of the seven plays below are bets on the part of a baseball game that a starting pitcher actually controls, and the reasoning underneath every one of them is the same. By late July, across a hundred games, the thing that separates these clubs is not the lineup. It is who keeps the ball in the yard for the first two turns through the order.

Then there is the seventh play, and it exists because the rule has an exception. In Minneapolis tonight, the two arms scheduled to start have the worst and third-worst ERAs of anyone pitching on this board, and the two bullpens behind them are just as leaky. When the men who are supposed to prevent runs cannot, you stop betting the pitching and you bet the scoreboard. Twelve and a half units, seven plays, one idea and its single honest exception. Every record, ERA, WHIP and run total below came out of the MLB Stats API this session. The prices and the stakes are the author's own card and come from no book feed.

Angels At Giants: The First Five Is The Whole Bet

Three units on the Giants to lead after five innings at minus 175, and the size is not a comment on San Francisco. The Giants are 42-60 with a 4.40 staff ERA and 481 runs allowed, and the bullpen behind Webb is exactly why this is a first-five bet and not a full-game moneyline. You do not want the last four innings of this team. You want the first five, and you want them because of who is standing in the other batter's box.

Grayson Rodriguez arrived in Anaheim over the winter, sent from Baltimore in the deal that shipped Taylor Ward the other way, and his first year as an Angel has been a wreck. He carries an 8.23 ERA and a 1.77 WHIP across 35 innings and eight starts, walking and hitting his way into trouble before the lineup ever gets a swing at anyone in relief. A first-five market exists to isolate the starters, and this one isolates a 3.87 ERA against an 8.23. Webb has thrown 107 innings with a 1.11 WHIP, the cleanest walk-and-hit rate of any pitcher working tonight, and he does his best work early, before a lineup has seen the sinker twice.

The counterpoint is the price itself. Minus 175 on five innings is a real toll, and Rodriguez has enough raw stuff that one clean turn through a bad Angels-facing order is not impossible. If Webb spots a two-run inning early and Rodriguez steals five zeroes, the ticket dies on a coin flip. That is the honest risk, and it is why three units and not five. What holds the play up is the gap. There is no pitcher on this board further from his opponent than Webb is from Rodriguez tonight.

A first-five moneyline throws away the bullpen and grades the starters head to head. Tonight that means Webb's 1.11 WHIP against a man carrying an 8.23 ERA, and nothing that happens after the fifth can touch the ticket.
Guardians At Rays: The Quietest Offense On The Card, Held Down Again

Two and a half units on the Cleveland team total under 3.5, the second-largest position of the night, and it is the surest thing on the board. The Guardians have scored 417 runs in 104 games, the quietest offense of any club playing tonight, and they walk into Tampa Bay against Shane McClanahan and his 3.16 ERA, 1.15 WHIP and 91 innings of work. A lineup that struggles to reach four runs against average pitching is being asked to reach four against one of the best arms it will see all month.

The same game supplies a smaller, related play: the game total under 7.5 for a unit and a half. Joey Cantillo has been steady for Cleveland at 3.74 over 106 innings, and two staffs sitting at 3.71 and 3.83 do not tend to combine for eight runs when the visiting lineup is the softest on the card. The team total is the sharper of the two because it depends on only one offense doing what it has done all year. The game total needs both bullpens to behave, and Tampa Bay's has 38 saves banked, the most of anyone here.

What beats the team total is the shape of McClanahan's misses. He has surrendered seven home runs in 91 innings, and Cleveland, for all its quiet, can run into one. A leadoff walk and a swing puts two on the board in a hurry, and suddenly a 3.5 is live into the seventh. That is the way this loses. It is still a play, because a .417-runs-per-game offense against a sub-1.20 WHIP arm is the exact matchup team-total unders are built for.

Reds At Cardinals: The Moneyline Behind The Cleaner Arm

Two units on St. Louis at minus 159, and this is the one straight moneyline where you are laying a real price at home. The Cardinals are 52-50 with a 4.18 staff ERA, and neither number is scary on its own. What makes the play is the arm on the other side. Rhett Lowder is 3-7 with a 5.75 ERA and a 1.64 WHIP over 72 innings, and a 1.64 WHIP means baserunners in bunches, inning after inning, against a lineup at home that only needs a few of them to score.

Dustin May is not an ace this year at 4.78, and it is worth being honest that his own 1.32 WHIP is not pristine. The edge is comparative, not absolute. May puts fewer men on than Lowder by a wide margin, works deeper, and hands the ball to a St. Louis bullpen that has converted 31 saves. Cincinnati sits at 47-54 with a 4.56 staff ERA and 423 runs scored, one of the lighter offenses on the card, which blunts the risk that May's own walks come back to bite.

The way this play dies is a May clunker. He has allowed seven home runs on the year and can give up a crooked number early, and if he does, a two-run hole against even a mediocre Reds bullpen is a bad place to be laying 159. One unit less than the Guardians total for that reason. The lineups and the WHIP gap point one direction. The starter's own inconsistency is the reason it is two units and not three.

Mariners At Rangers: A Team Total Behind The Best WHIP On The Board

One unit on the Texas team total under 3.5, and the whole case is one number. Bryce Miller has a 0.88 WHIP. Not for a month, for the season, across 63 and a third innings and ten starts, with a 2.27 ERA to match. A WHIP under 0.90 means he is walking almost no one and allowing fewer than a hit an inning, and a Rangers lineup that has scored 427 runs is not the group to break that open. Texas at home against Miller reaching four runs is the bet you are fading.

MacKenzie Gore starts for Seattle at 4.80, which does not matter to this ticket, because a team total under is a bet on the pitcher your team is facing, not the one it is sending out. The relevant fact is that Seattle's staff ERA is 3.59, second best on the entire card, and Miller is its sharpest edge tonight. Texas scoring three or fewer is a live, ordinary outcome against that.

The reason it is only a unit is Miller's innings count. Sixty-three and a third across ten starts is a light season, the sign of a pitcher who has been managed carefully or missed time, and a short leash means the Texas lineup could be into a Seattle bullpen by the sixth. If that pen leaks a couple of late runs, a 3.5 clears on garbage time. Miller's brilliance is real. His workload is the asterisk, and it caps the size.

Yankees At Phillies: A Plus Price On The Best Run Prevention In The League

One unit on New York at plus 122, and the case starts with the arm the market is somehow discounting. Cam Schlittler has been the quiet best of this rotation, a 2.20 ERA and a 0.96 WHIP across 123 innings, with 145 strikeouts and fewer than a baserunner an inning. That is a sharper line than the man he is matched against. Jesus Luzardo is very good, 9-4 with a 3.43 ERA and 143 strikeouts over 115 innings, but on ERA, on WHIP and on hits allowed, the road starter is the better of the two tonight.

So why the plus number. It is a road game, and the price shades toward the home side and toward a Phillies club sitting at 56-47. That is the inefficiency. The Yankees have allowed 392 runs, the fewest of any club on the board, behind a 3.38 staff ERA that is the best of the twelve teams playing tonight. Put the better starter in front of the better bullpen and the better run differential, and getting paid a plus number to back all three is a price that tends to shorten once lineups are posted.

What beats it is the ballpark and one swing. Citizens Bank Park plays small, Schlittler has already surrendered twelve home runs, and Luzardo owns the kind of strikeout stuff that flips a low-scoring game with a single clean six-inning turn. A 123-inning workload by late July is a lot of mileage on a young arm, and one crooked frame in that yard is all it takes when you are laying nothing and collecting plus money. That is why it is one unit, not three. The edge is real. The margin is thin.

Athletics At Twins: The One Place The Arms Quit

A unit and a half on the game total over 9.5, and this is the exception that defines the rest of the card. Everywhere else tonight the play backs the pitching. Here there is no pitching to back. Jacob Lopez starts for the Athletics with a 6.64 ERA and a 1.73 WHIP, and Zebby Matthews answers for Minnesota with a 5.40 ERA and fifteen home runs allowed in seventy innings. Those are the worst and the near-worst starters on the entire board, matched against each other in a park that has already seen plenty of traffic this year.

The bullpens do not save it. Minnesota's staff ERA is 4.71 and the Athletics' is 5.39, the single worst mark of any club pitching tonight, and both offenses can hit. The Twins have scored 498 runs, more than anyone else on this card, and the Athletics have 458 of their own. Put two of the leakiest staffs in the league in front of two lineups that are not the problem, and 9.5 is a number the game clears on ordinary contact.

The way an over dies is the way it always does, a pair of surprise gems or an early rain-shortened script, and there is nothing here suggesting either. This is not a clever bet. It is the honest read that when both starters own an ERA north of five and both bullpens are the worst on the board, you stop pretending run prevention is on offer and you bet the runs. One idea, one exception, both true on the same card.

Twelve And A Half Units, One Idea

The card is a single sentence with one footnote. Webb over Rodriguez for the first five is the gap between a 3.87 ERA and an 8.23, graded before the bullpen ever throws a pitch. The Guardians team total under 3.5 takes the quietest offense in the field against McClanahan's 1.15 WHIP, and the game under 7.5 rides the same starter for a smaller stake. The Cardinals moneyline lays 159 because Dustin May puts far fewer men on than a 1.64-WHIP Lowder. The Rangers team total under trusts Bryce Miller's 0.88 WHIP to keep a home lineup under four. The Yankees at plus 122 buy the best run prevention in the American League as a road dog. And then Minnesota, where both starters and both bullpens are the worst arms on the board, and the only sane read is the over. Six bets on the men who prevent runs, one bet on the two who cannot. Nobody sells tickets to watch a starter go five and hand off a lead. That is exactly why the market keeps leaving the edge in the first five innings.
