MLB Scoring Trends

Since The Break, Four Offenses Went Cold And Four Caught Fire

July 25, 2026 | MLB Second-Half Scoring Trends | Sports Betting Prime

The Washington Nationals are averaging 7.57 runs a game since the All-Star break. The Toronto Blue Jays are averaging 2.13. Both numbers come from the same nine days of baseball, the same run-scoring environment, the same 30-team league, and they sit at opposite ends of a split that has quietly taken over the sport since play resumed on July 17. Four offenses have gone ice cold. Four have caught fire. And on Saturday night, several of them are on the same schedule, in the same buildings, against each other.

This is not a betting card and it is not a claim that any of these eight teams keeps doing what it has done for the last week and a half. Seven to nine games is a small window, the kind that gets erased by one bad Tuesday or one three-homer night, and the honest version of this piece ends with that warning stated plainly rather than buried. It is, however, a real and verifiable split, pulled directly from this season's game logs, and it happens to line up with tonight's schedule closely enough that it is worth laying out before the caveat.

The Cold Four

No team on the list has fallen further in raw terms than the Yankees. New York is 58-45 and averaging 4.67 runs a game across a 103-game season built on one of the more expensive lineups in the sport, and 2.71 across the seven games since the break, a drop of exactly two runs a night. The Yankees play the Phillies tonight at Citizens Bank Park. It is not part of the clean hot-cold pairings elsewhere on the board, but it is worth watching for its own reason: a cold offense gets a real look at finding out whether the cold is real against a Philadelphia staff that, at 4.31 runs a game allowed itself, is not built to hand much back.

Toronto has actually been colder in rate terms. The Blue Jays, 48-57, are averaging 2.13 runs a game since July 17 against a 3.95 season mark that was already middling, the single lowest post-break number of any team in the sport. Los Angeles' Angels, 41-64 and buried in the AL West, have fallen from 4.29 to 2.57. And Miami, 52-53, sits at 2.86 runs a game since the break against a 4.39 season average, the fourth name on the cold list and the one that matters most on tonight's schedule, because the Marlins are hosting the hottest lineup in the National League.

New York has scored two fewer runs a game since the break than it did across the first three and a half months of its season. That is not a slump measured in strikeouts. It is a full run environment disappearing for a lineup that was, until nine days ago, one of the better ones in the league.
The Hot Four

Nowhere on tonight's schedule is the split more direct than Washington, where the Diamondbacks arrive carrying a hot streak of their own. Arizona, 55-50, is averaging 6.63 runs a game since the break against a 4.50 season mark, and the Nationals, 53-52, have been the single hottest offense anywhere in the sport over the same stretch at 7.57, up from a season average that was already a healthy 5.47. Two offenses running that far above their own baseline, in the same building, on the same night, is not a matchup where either probable starter is really the headline. Mitch Bratt throws for Arizona and Foster Griffin for Washington, and neither man's season line explains why this total should trade where a cold-versus-cold total normally would.

Ninety miles from where the Yankees are trying to find their season again, the Padres land in Miami running just as hot as Washington and Arizona. San Diego, 52-53, is averaging 6.38 runs a game since July 17, up from 4.16 across the full season, and it lands squarely on a Marlins lineup that has scored 2.86 runs a night over the same stretch. That is the cleanest hot-versus-cold pairing on the entire board tonight, a two-week version of San Diego's best offensive stretch of the season against a Miami lineup that has not found more than a couple of runs a game since the break resumed. Randy Vasquez starts for the Padres, Eury Perez for the Marlins.

The fourth hot team does not have a cold-list opponent tonight. Atlanta, 61-42 and the best record of anyone on this list, is in Baltimore at 6.88 runs a game since the break, up from an already strong 5.00 mark on the season. Bryce Elder gets the ball against Brandon Young in a game that is really just the Braves trying to keep doing what they have done for eight days, not part of the direct collisions happening in Washington and Miami.

The Honest Caveat

None of this is a bet, and the reason is worth stating in plain numbers rather than a disclaimer. Seven, eight and nine games is not a sample that survives contact with a 162-game season, and every team on this list has produced a stretch like it before, in both directions, that reverted within two weeks. The Nationals were a below-average offense for most of June before this stretch. The Yankees carried a top-five scoring rate in May. Regression toward a team's real level is the single most reliable trend in baseball, and the honest read of this split is that it will look meaningfully different by the middle of August, possibly before the week is out.

What the split is useful for tonight is context, not a pick. When a team total or a game total looks unusually short or unusually long on the board, a run of games like this is one real, checkable reason why, and knowing whether a number reflects nine games of noise or a genuine change in a lineup is the first question worth asking before deciding whether that number is sharp or just recent.

Eight Teams, One Split, One Warning

Cold: Yankees, Blue Jays, Angels, Marlins. Hot: Nationals, Diamondbacks, Padres, Braves. Two of the four collisions on tonight's schedule put a hot team directly against a cold one or another hot one, and that is closer to a scheduling coincidence than a storyline anyone planned, but the underlying split is real and measurable back to the exact day the second half started. Whether it survives the next two weeks is the one part of this article that cannot be checked against a stats feed. Ask again in mid-August.
