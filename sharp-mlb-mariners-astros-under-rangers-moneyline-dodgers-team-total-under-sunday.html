Seattle And Houston Scored 32 Runs. That Night The Card Says Under.

Historical · not a current board

This was the board as of 2026-08-16. Any price shown below is the price that was available at that time, not a current quote. The original analysis is unchanged; present-tense wording has been corrected so nothing reads as live.

Seattle and Houston have played twice this weekend. Friday they combined for 17 runs. Saturday they combined for 15. Thirty-two runs in two nights between the same two lineups, and that night the same two teams meet a third time with a fresh pair of arms, and the card's smallest stake says the total goes under seven and a half.

That is the tension running through all three plays on that day's fifteen-game board. Three units are out, split across a moneyline in Sacramento, a total in Houston, and a team total in Los Angeles, and each one is betting that a single Sunday performance breaks a pattern the last several days have been building against it.

Texas Rangers Moneyline (-132), 1.5 Units

Cody Bradford gets the ball for Texas with almost nothing behind him. Two starts, 11.1 innings, a 2.38 ERA and a 1.15 WHIP, and a strikeout rate so low it barely registers: six punchouts against three walks, 4.76 per nine. He allowed five hits and no earned runs across 4.1 innings against San Francisco on August 5, then gave up three earned runs in seven innings against the Angels on August 11. Neither start tells you much on its own. Together they say a pitcher who is not missing bats but has, so far, kept the ball in the yard and the line clean.

Jacob deGrom is not on the mound that night, Cody Bradford is, but deGrom's rotation is the same Texas staff facing an Athletics club that is 22-40 at home this year.

Jacob Lopez counters for the Athletics, and his season line, 5-4 with a 5.38 ERA over 82 innings and 44 walks against 75 strikeouts, undersells what he has done lately. Over his last four starts, from July 24 through August 10, Lopez has thrown 22.1 innings and allowed five earned runs, an ERA near 2.00 in that window, striking out 26 against eight walks. That is the single biggest threat to this ticket. A pitcher trending this sharply can beat a good lineup on a bad staff on any given night, regardless of what his April and May looked like.

What argues for Texas anyway is the record, and it is not close. The Athletics are 22-40 at home this season, the worst mark in the American League West by a wide margin, despite a home OPS of .788 that is nearly 150 points higher than their .644 mark on the road. A team that hits at its own park and still loses two of every three games there is describing its pitching staff, not its lineup, and Lopez's four good starts are one man against a larger pattern. Texas is 29-36 on the road, itself a losing record, but the Rangers won the last meeting between these two clubs, 5-3 on Saturday, a night after losing 8-3 in the opener of this same series.

The Athletics have hit at a .788 home OPS and still gone 22-40 at their own park. Jacob Lopez's last four starts are the reason this line sits at -132 and not lower.

At -132 the ticket needs 56.9 percent. Texas scores 4.07 runs a game across the full season, 4.03 on the road, both middling numbers that do not make the case by themselves. The case is that the Athletics have found a way to lose at home against far worse teams than this one, and Lopez's recent form has to hold for a third straight turn to be the exception rather than the rule.

Astros/Mariners Under 7.5 (-105), 1 Unit

Start with the box scores, because they argue directly against this ticket. Seattle and Houston have already played twice this weekend at Daikin Park. Friday's game finished 10-7 Houston, 17 total runs. Saturday's finished 10-5 Houston, 15 total runs. Neither number is close to 7.5, and both happened in the same building where that night's game is played, against a Houston pitching staff that has clearly had trouble containing this particular Seattle lineup over the last two days.

The broader Houston home split does not help the case either. The Astros have played 61 home games this season, and only 22 of them, 36.1 percent, have finished with a combined total of seven runs or fewer. Thirty-nine of 61 have gone to eight or more. On a raw season basis, Astros home games clear this number nearly two times out of three.

Bryan Woo and Hunter Brown make the argument for the under, two pitchers capable of shortening a game by themselves when they are on. Woo's last four starts have been uneven, five earned runs against Texas on July 25, five more against the Dodgers on July 30, then back to back scoreless outings against Detroit and the Yankees on August 5 and August 11, seven innings and five innings respectively without allowing a run. His season line reads 4.15 with a 1.10 WHIP and 132 strikeouts in 132.1 innings, a strikeout an inning most of the year. Brown has been shorter in his outings, 63.2 innings across 12 starts, but his stuff plays: 67 strikeouts, a 9.47 K/9, and a run of four straight starts allowing three earned runs or fewer, most recently three runs in five innings against the Giants on August 11.

The problem with leaning on Brown specifically is command. He has walked 35 batters in 63.2 innings this season, a 4.95 BB/9, and free baserunners are exactly how an inning turns into a five-run inning against a Seattle lineup that has already shown it can do damage against Houston twice this week. At -105 the number needed is a modest 51.2 percent, the easiest break-even on the card, but the two most recent, most direct data points available, the actual games these two teams just played against each other, both blew well past it.

Dodgers Team Total Under 4.5 (-140), 0.5 Units

This is the smallest stake and the cleanest number. Los Angeles has hosted Milwaukee three times already this series, and the Dodgers have scored four, three and one runs in those three games, going 1-2 while doing it. All three totals clear this number. Zoom out to the full season and the pattern holds: in 62 home games, the Dodgers have scored four runs or fewer 40 times, 64.5 percent, comfortably above the 58.3 percent this price requires.

Logan Henderson is the specific reason, and his season has been close to dominant. A 2.88 ERA, a 0.91 WHIP, 69 strikeouts against 11 walks in 56.1 innings, an 11.02 K/9 with a walk rate that stays out of his own way. His last four starts read 5.0 innings and two earned runs against the Mets, 5.2 scoreless against the Giants, six innings and two earned against Pittsburgh, and 6.1 innings and three earned against San Diego on August 10. He has not walked more than two batters in any of those outings. A pitcher who limits both hard contact and free baserunners is the profile that keeps a good offense under its own number.

There is an odd wrinkle behind this game that has nothing to do with the bet directly but explains part of why Los Angeles keeps landing under its own home number. The Dodgers score 5.53 runs a game on the road this season against only 4.37 at home, a reversal of the usual home field bump. Whatever is behind it, unfamiliar mound dimensions, a deeper pitching staff seen more often at home, simple variance, the effect has been real and consistent enough to show up in both the season split and the last three games against this same opponent. Tarik Skubal takes the mound for Los Angeles that night in just his third start since a midseason trade from Detroit, 11 innings, five earned runs and 12 strikeouts across his first two outings in Dodger blue, but that assignment works against Milwaukee's bats, not the Dodgers' own team total, and is mentioned here for context rather than as part of the case.

At -140 the number needed is 58.3 percent, and the season rate plus a perfect 3-for-3 stretch against this same Milwaukee staff both sit above it. The honest risk is sample size. Three games is three games, and Henderson has not faced this exact Los Angeles lineup enough times to rule out one loud inning turning a comfortable 0.5-unit play into a quick loss.

Where The Model Lands

An independent engine run on the same fifteen-game board agrees with one of these three plays directly and offers a mixed read on the other two. For Texas at Oakland, the model sets the total at 10.5 and projects the Athletics for 5.21 runs against 6.97 for the Rangers, a split that favors Texas without directly pricing the moneyline. For Seattle at Houston, the model lands almost exactly on the market number, 7.5, projecting Houston for 4.21 and Seattle for 3.38, a combined 7.59, essentially a coin flip on the under and not the strong signal the card would want given how the last two games actually went. The clearest agreement is Los Angeles, where the model's own Dodgers projection of 4.12 runs sits below the 4.5 team total, and the fact sheet behind that day's board flags this as the single standalone qualifying signal the engine produced from the full slate. The same offense filter that generates that signal also blocked the model from betting unders on the Cubs and Tigers that day, both clubs sitting in the top fifth of the league in rolling 30-day scoring, which is the kind of guardrail that keeps a model from betting against a lineup that is actively hot.

What Beats This Card

The clearest threat sits with the Astros-Mariners under, and it is not subtle: the two teams on this ticket have combined for 32 runs in their last two head to head games, both played in the same ballpark this bet is riding on that night. If Woo or Brown has an off night, or if Brown's command issues put runners on base the way they have all season, the total clears 7.5 by the fourth inning. The Rangers moneyline dies if Jacob Lopez's hot stretch continues into a third straight strong start, since a pitcher this locked in can beat a mediocre lineup regardless of what the rest of his roster has done at home all year. The Dodgers team total is the safest math on the board, but it is the smallest sample too, three games against one pitching staff, and one uncharacteristic walk-filled inning from Henderson turns a 0.5-unit lean into a loss. None of the three plays needs a career night to cash. All three need the version of each pitcher and each team that has shown up most often since late July to show up one more time.
