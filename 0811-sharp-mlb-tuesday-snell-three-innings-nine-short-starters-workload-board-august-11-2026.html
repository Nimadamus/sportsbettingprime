Blake Snell Has Thrown Three Innings This Year. He Starts Tonight.

August 11, 2026 | MLB | Market Analysis, No Plays | Sports Betting Prime

Three innings. Four earned runs. One appearance, on May 9, against Atlanta. That is the entirety of Blake Snell's 2026 season, and tonight the Los Angeles Dodgers hand him the ball against Kansas City with a 71-48 record behind him.

Fifteen games are scheduled. Twenty-nine starters have been named, because Washington still has not posted an arm against Shota Imanaga. Nine of those twenty-nine have thrown fewer than 65 innings in 2026. Stack all nine of them together and the pile reaches 287 and a third innings, which is less than Cristopher Sanchez and Michael Wacha have thrown between the two of them tonight. Two arms outweigh nine.

Boards get read through records and prices. This one is better read through a workload column that nobody puts on the screen.

Ninety-Four Days

Snell's career line is the resume of a front-of-rotation starter: 1,161 innings, a 3.17 ERA, 1,445 strikeouts across 223 starts. His 2026 line is three innings and four earned runs on May 9. Ninety-four days have passed since he last threw a competitive pitch, and the club sending him out tonight shares the best record in the National League.

Across the diamond stands the opposite case. Michael Wacha has made 23 starts and logged 144 innings with a 3.44 ERA for a Kansas City club sitting 49-71, and the Royals score 4.14 runs per game. Wacha has been available every fifth day all year for a team going nowhere. Snell has been unavailable almost the entire season for a team going everywhere. Both facts are on tonight's card at the same time.

Paul Skenes starts at Miami tonight carrying 129.2 innings and 162 strikeouts in 2026, the other end of the workload board.

The Other Eight

Cody Bradford has thrown four and a third innings this year, all of them on August 5 against San Francisco, and he allowed nothing. Texas sends him to Anaheim against Ryan Johnson, a 24-year-old with a 7.11 ERA and 50.2 innings whose last six turns include a three and a third inning outing that cost eight earned runs. Neither of those pitchers has a sixth inning in him tonight barring something unusual, and the sixth inning is where this game gets decided.

Boston's spot is stranger. Patrick Sandoval has five starts and 24 innings back from a long absence, a 1.67 WHIP inside those 24 innings, and a career that already holds 560 innings and 45 losses. He is opposed at Rogers Centre by Dylan Cease, who owns a 2.28 ERA, 184 strikeouts in 126.1 innings and a .179 opponent batting average. That is one of the sharpest full-season pitching lines in the league standing across from a man who has faced major league hitters five times since spring.

San Francisco's Carson Whisenhunt has 22.1 innings and a 7.25 ERA, and his last two starts lasted three and a third innings apiece for a combined ten earned runs. Mitch Bratt, 23 years old, has 27.2 major league innings in his entire life, every one of them thrown this season, with 18 walks inside them. He also went seven shutout innings with nine strikeouts at San Diego on August 5, which is the single best start any of these nine arms has produced.

Hunter Brown belongs on the list by innings and nowhere near it by quality. Eleven starts, 58.2 innings, a 3.53 ERA and a .196 opponent average for Houston. Short is not the same as bad.

Nine short starters, 287 and a third innings between them. Sanchez and Wacha have 293 and two thirds tonight without any help.
The Record That Lies Loudest

Tanner Bibee takes the ball at Comerica Park with a 4-11 record. He also has a 3.85 ERA and a 1.13 WHIP over 138 innings and 24 starts, which is a perfectly good season attached to a won-lost line that reads like a disaster. Cleveland scores 3.97 runs per game, third from the bottom in the majors. Bibee's record is a hitting statistic wearing a pitcher's name.

Washington's slot deserves the same suspicion in reverse. The Nationals lead all of Major League Baseball at 5.36 runs per game, ahead of the Cubs at 5.16, and they sit 59-61. Their starter tonight against Imanaga still has not been announced.

The Position

The read here is that tonight's board separates cleanly into two kinds of game, and the split has nothing to do with the standings. In nine of these fifteen, at least one starter is working on a short leash by the weight of his own season, which pushes the decisive innings into a bullpen that has not been announced, cannot be scouted from a schedule page, and does not appear in any of the season-long numbers people quote at each other.

Paul Skenes at 129.2 innings, Cristopher Sanchez at 149.2 with a 2.65 ERA and a 15-4 record, Nick Martinez at 129 innings and a 2.65 ERA for Tampa Bay, Martin Perez at 35 years old with 1,732 career innings behind him: those games have a shape you can actually project. The Snell game does not. The Bradford game does not. Treating the two categories as the same exercise is the mistake.

The Honest Counterpoint

Every one of these innings totals is public, and no oddsmaker is unaware that Blake Snell has thrown three innings. The counts are in the projection systems, they are in the pitcher props, and they were priced into the number before it ever posted.

There is a second, better objection. A starter with 22 innings on the year and a hard cap in front of him can be a much safer bet than a workhorse dragging 150 innings into August on legs that have felt every one of them. Bratt's seven shutout innings on August 5 came from the arm with the fewest career innings on this entire board. A fresh pitcher and a rested bullpen is a perfectly ordinary way to hold a team to two runs. Reading a low innings total as an automatic argument for runs is a shortcut, and shortcuts get priced out of markets faster than anything else.
