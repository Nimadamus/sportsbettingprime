Sharp MLB Saturday

Four Unders On The Board, Four Different Reasons

July 25, 2026 | MLB Team Total and Game Total Reads | Sports Betting Prime

The Mets placed Juan Soto on the injured list Saturday morning with a strained left calf, and by the time Citi Field opened its gates for early batting practice, DraftKings and Caesars were the only two sportsbooks still sitting on a Dodgers-Mets game total of 8. Everywhere else, the number had already been walked down to 7.5. That is what a total looks like once the market has finished pricing a fact the public is still catching up on, and it is the cleanest single data point on a Saturday board carrying four separate unders, each one moving for a reason that has nothing to do with a favorite.

Six units are live across four plays tonight, and every one of them is a bet that a lineup falls short of a number rather than a bet that a team wins. The lazy read is to treat that as one long correlated fade of National League offense. That read is wrong. A Mets team total, a Dodgers-Mets game total, a Cubs team total and a Mariners-Rangers game total are each protecting against something structurally different: a lineup that lost its best hitter three hours before first pitch, a total that opened wide and has already been bet down once, an ace turning a top-five scoring offense into a home underdog, and a market that, on the fourth game, cannot agree with itself on which side deserves the vig. Take them one at a time.

Dodgers At Mets: The Injury The Board Had Already Priced

Soto's exit is not a graze. He is hitting .293 with 21 home runs and 52 RBI in 84 games, the best bat in a Mets lineup that has otherwise scored 421 runs in 104 games this season, a below-average 4.05 runs a night. New York has already lived through what a Soto absence does to this offense once: a right calf strain in April cost him 15 games, and the Mets went 3-12 without him. Now it is the left calf, an initial recovery estimate in the four-to-six week range, and the club has already recalled infielder-outfielder Eric Wagaman from Triple-A Syracuse to fill the roster spot.

The total told you the market noticed before most of the field did. Dodgers-Mets opened at 8. By Saturday afternoon it had been bet down to 7.5 almost everywhere, and the only two books still parked on the opening number were DraftKings and Caesars. If the game under at minus 125 is the right side, and Yamamoto's season says it is, the actual value in this exact matchup sits at whichever of those two books hasn't caught up yet, not at the shops that already moved. Yamamoto carries a 2.77 ERA and a 0.89 WHIP across 120.1 innings this year, 10-6 with 113 strikeouts, the best walk-and-hit rate of any starter throwing tonight. Nolan McLean has been good in his own right for the Mets, a 3.31 ERA and 135 strikeouts over 114.1 innings, but he has also walked 41 batters in that span, more than triple Yamamoto's total across comparable work, and a start that leaks free baserunners is the one live way this under gets uncomfortable.

Dodgers-Mets opened at 8 and moved to 7.5 almost everywhere by Saturday afternoon. Two books had not caught up. That gap, not a hunch, is the actual edge on the game total tonight.

The same information prices the Mets team total under 3.5 at minus 145, a far steeper number than any other play on tonight's board. That gap between minus 145 here and minus 111 on the Cubs below is the market telling you plainly how much of the Soto news is already baked in. This is not a ground-floor read. It is a bet that an offense already averaging just over four runs a night, now without its best hitter, and facing one of the better qualifying ERAs in the National League, fails to reach four. The book knows that too, which is exactly why it costs 45 cents to lay here instead of 11.

Cubs At Pirates: Paying To Bet Against The Fourth-Best Offense In Baseball

The Cubs are 58-45 and have scored 523 runs in 103 games, 5.08 a night, the fourth-highest scoring rate in Major League Baseball. They are also plus-117 underdogs on the moneyline tonight at PNC Park, and the reason both facts are true at once is standing on the mound in a Pirates uniform. Paul Skenes carries a 3.35 ERA and a minuscule 1.03 WHIP across 118.1 innings, with 144 strikeouts against just 27 walks in 22 starts, and Pittsburgh has priced its own moneyline down near minus 122 to minus 132 on the back of one arm carrying an otherwise middling team.

A unit and a half on the Cubs team total under 3.5 at minus 111 is a bet on exactly that arm and nothing else. It costs less than a third of the vig on the Mets play above because there is no lineup subtraction doing the work here, no injury the market has already absorbed. This is a straight bet that a top-five scoring offense in the sport does not clear three and a half runs against a pitcher who has allowed fewer than a baserunner an inning all season. PNC Park itself is not helping the case. Its 2026 run factor sits at a neutral 1.00, with home runs suppressed slightly at 0.98, meaning the park is not why anyone would take this number. Skenes is. It is also an open-air riverside park prone to wind swings that have turned a clean start into a crooked inning before, and that is the honest way this one loses: not a Cubs offense finding its rhythm, but one bad-luck sequence off a starter who has already thrown 118 innings and is not walking through late July unscathed.

Mariners At Rangers: The Total Even The Books Cannot Agree On

Every play above has a clear driver: an ace, an injury, a market that has already moved. The Mariners-Rangers under 7.5 does not have one of those, and that is worth saying plainly before laying the unit. FanDuel currently prices the over at minus 122 and the under at plus 100 on the same 7.5, a 22-cent gap on a mainline total that normally trades inside a dime. A spread that wide means the number is thin, not settled, and a thin number cuts both ways.

The offenses support the under on paper. Seattle is averaging 4.03 runs a night across 104 games and Texas 4.19 across 103, both bottom-third marks in the league, and neither Bryan Woo (4.23 ERA, 1.11 WHIP) nor Nathan Eovaldi (4.18 ERA, 1.19 WHIP, 22 home runs allowed in 116.1 innings) is doing anything close to what Yamamoto or Skenes did above. This is a bet on two average offenses staying average against two merely functional arms, not a bet on dominance.

The park adds a genuine complication instead of clearing one up. Globe Life Field's full-season run factor sits at 1.07, mildly hitter-friendly, though that number is dragged toward neutral by a roof that stays closed for more than 80 percent of Rangers home dates. In late-July Texas heat, tonight's roof is almost certainly shut, which argues for the more neutral read, but it is not the tailwind the Cubs and Mets unders got from a genuinely dominant start. One unit here, the smallest stake of the four, because the case is real but thinner than anywhere else on the board, and the split price is the market admitting the same thing.

Six Units, Four Reasons

Stack the four plays end to end and the total risk is six units, and the instinct to treat that as one big correlated under is understandable but wrong. The Mets number exists because a lineup lost its best hitter this morning and the board has already priced it at minus 145. The Dodgers-Mets game number exists because Yamamoto is pitching the way his ERA says he is pitching, and two books have not caught up to a total that moved from 8 to 7.5 everywhere else. The Cubs number exists because Paul Skenes is having the kind of season that turns a fourth-best offense in the sport into a road underdog, priced at a fraction of the Mets play because there is no lineup crutch doing the work. And the Rangers-Mariners number exists mostly because two average offenses are meeting two functional arms in a building the market itself cannot fully agree on. Four unders, four different reasons, and one thing worth remembering before the first pitch: the size of each play already reflects how much of the edge is still live versus how much the board took away before anyone got to it.
