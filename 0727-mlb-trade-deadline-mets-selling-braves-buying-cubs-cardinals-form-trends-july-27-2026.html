The Team Trading Away Its Bullpen Just Found Its Best Arm

Historical · not a current board

This was the board as of the date shown. Any price shown below is the price that was available at that time, not a current quote. The original analysis is unchanged; present-tense wording has been corrected so nothing reads as live.

Zac Thornton threw six shutout innings against Milwaukee on July 21, three hits, two walks, two strikeouts, and got tagged with a no-decision anyway because the New York Mets bullpen he pitched in front of gave it back. It was his fourth career start. He is 24 years old, he carries a 1.93 ERA through 23.1 innings, and he is not going anywhere before the trade deadline. Almost everyone else on that roster might be. The Mets are 44-62, in last place in the National League East, and are expected to make at least six trades before the August 3 deadline arrives. Monday night, Thornton's team hosts a Braves club playing the best baseball of anyone on that night's twelve-game slate, and the gap between those two rosters is really a gap between two different plans for the rest of the season.

Two games on the July 27 board sit on opposite sides of that same calendar. One is Braves at Mets, a clean split between a buyer and a seller. The other, Cubs at Cardinals in St. Louis, is messier: a team outplaying its own full-season record by a wide margin against a team that still somehow leads their season series. Records tell you who is buying and who is selling. The numbers from the last ten days tell you something the standings alone will not.

Braves At Mets: A Buyer And A Seller Share A Field

Atlanta is 62-43 and has been the hottest team in either game on that night's board, seven wins in its last ten with 60 runs scored in that stretch, six a night. That run included a 15-run outburst on July 17 and four separate games of six runs or more. Martin Perez starts for the Braves in his 16th outing of the season, 6-6 with a 3.38 ERA, a 1.20 WHIP and 65 strikeouts across 85.1 innings, the kind of steady mid-rotation season that lets a front office spend the next week adding rather than subtracting.

New York is doing the opposite. At 44-62 the Mets sit in last place in the NL East, and multiple independent reports this week name Freddy Peralta as the most likely player to be dealt before August 3, with Clay Holmes, Brooks Raley and Tyrone Taylor also expected to draw real interest as the team's front office works toward at least six separate trades. Against that backdrop, Thornton's start is the one piece of the roster nobody is shopping. His last two outings read 7 innings and zero earned runs against Boston on July 12, then 6 innings and zero earned runs against Milwaukee on July 21, back to back scoreless starts from a pitcher who has made four career appearances. The Mets have gone 4-5 over their last nine games and scored 3.78 runs a night in that stretch, a middling number for a team about to be picked apart. Thornton's ERA is the one part of the roster moving in the opposite direction from everything around him.

Zac Thornton has thrown 13 innings and allowed zero earned runs across his last two starts, for a team expected to trade away most of its bullpen before August 3. He is 24, he has four career starts, and he is the one arm on that roster nobody is shopping.
Cubs At Cardinals: The Record Says One Thing, The Last Ten Days Say Another

Chicago is 59-46, St. Louis 53-52, a six-game gap that undersells how differently these two teams have actually played lately. Over their last ten games the Cubs are 6-4 with a .273 team batting average, a 3.26 ERA and a plus-28 run differential. The Cardinals, over the same stretch, are 3-7 with a .223 batting average and a 4.18 ERA. That is not a marginal split. It is one team hitting and pitching well across the board while the other struggles at nearly every phase of the game, and yet St. Louis still holds a 4-2 edge in their season series, all six meetings already played before that night's seventh.

The pitching matchup does not obviously explain the gap either. David Peterson starts for the Cubs at 5-7 with a 5.97 ERA and a 1.56 WHIP across 89 innings, a rough season line by any measure. Matthew Liberatore goes for the Cardinals at 5-7 with a 5.18 ERA and a 1.52 WHIP over 99 innings, marginally better than Peterson on paper. Neither starter is the reason Chicago has outscored its opponents by 28 runs in ten days while St. Louis has come apart at the plate. That is a lineup and bullpen story more than a starting pitching one, and it is exactly the kind of gap a six-game standings difference does not fully capture. The market has Chicago as only modest favorites, and a book pricing a game close after one side has clearly outplayed the other for ten straight days is worth watching, even without a specific play attached to it.

What The Deadline Calendar Does And Does Not Tell You

Institutional direction and recent form are two different measurements, and that night's board has one game where they agree and one where they do not. Atlanta is buying and playing well; New York is selling and playing an ordinary brand of .500-ish baseball around one genuinely excellent young arm who is not part of the sale. That is the tidy version of a trade deadline story, a good team getting better and a bad team getting realistic about itself. Chicago and St. Louis do not resolve as cleanly. The Cubs have been the better team by a wide margin for the last ten days and still show up as only slight favorites that night, in a season series their opponent already leads. A standings page updates once a game. A last-ten-days column updates just as often and tells a different, faster-moving story, and both are true about the same team at the same time.
