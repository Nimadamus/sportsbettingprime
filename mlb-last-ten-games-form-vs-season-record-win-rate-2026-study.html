In 2026 A Team Five Or More Wins Hotter Over Its Last Ten Won 53.6 Percent, Less Than A Team One Win Hotter

Every broadcast shows it and every preview leans on it: the last ten games. A team that's 9 and 1 sounds like a team you want, and a team that's 1 and 9 sounds like one you fade. So we went back through the 2026 season and asked a plain question. When one team's last ten was better than its opponent's, how often did it win the next game, and did a bigger edge in recent form buy a higher win rate?

It didn't. A team with just one more win over its last ten won 54.4 percent of 621 games. A team with five or more extra wins won 53.6 percent of 138. The hotter edge did slightly worse. Run the same test on the season record instead and the line climbs the way you'd expect, from 52.2 percent at the smallest gaps to 56.2 percent at .150 or more.

What we measured

2,134 completed regular season games from April 6 through September 16, 2026, every game in which both teams had already played at least ten. All 2,286 final scores from Opening Day on were replayed in date order, so each team's last ten and its season record are exactly what they were before first pitch. A game where both teams had the same last ten is left out of the form table, which is why that table covers 1,802 games.

When form and record disagree, form loses its voice

This is the cut that settles it. In 1,275 games the hotter team was also the team with the better season record, and it won 55.1 percent. In 497 games the hotter team had the worse season record. It won 247 of them, 49.7 percent. When the form edge was four wins or more and the record still pointed the other way, the hot team won 23 of 47. A streak didn't beat the season. It barely showed up.

Where a cold stretch does show up: runs

Winning is one thing. Scoring is another, and here the last ten tells you a little more. Across the study window a team scored four runs or fewer in 56.7 percent of its games. Split by how that team had done over its last ten:

A cold team scored about a quarter of a run less and stayed at four or fewer three and a half points more often. Put that cold team at home against a visitor who had won seven or more of its last ten and it scored four or fewer in 60 of 96 games, 62.5 percent. It's a real lean, on a sample that's modest rather than large.

Padres ML at -195. This is the extreme version of the form story. San Diego is 9 and 1 over its last ten and Colorado is 1 and 9, an eight win gap, and the Padres took two of three at Coors to open this series. The study says that gap is close to noise. Road teams with a season record edge of .150 or more won 105 of 191, 55.0 percent. Add a form edge of five or more and it's 20 of 35, 57.1 percent. Neither is anywhere near 66.10. So if this ticket is right, the reason isn't the standings or the streak. It's the matchup on the mound. Michael King has a 3.03 ERA over 175 innings and allowed no earned runs in three of his last four starts. Tanner Gordon has a 5.55 ERA, a .884 OPS against, and a 7.47 ERA in six home starts at Coors. San Diego has won 10 of 12 against Colorado this season. That's the case, and it's a pitching case, not a form case.

White Sox team total under 4.5 at -145. Chicago is 3 and 7 over its last ten and Detroit is 7 and 3, which puts the White Sox exactly in the cold home team against a hot visitor row: 62.5 percent of 96 games at four runs or fewer, about three points over the 59.18 break even. The broader cold team row, 59.6 percent, sits right on it. Framber Valdez brings a 4.01 ERA but hasn't allowed an earned run in 13 innings over his last two starts. The pushback is real and worth saying out loud: the White Sox hit better at home, a .743 OPS, and they scored seven off Valdez in Chicago on May 30. They've scored four or fewer in 37 of 74 home games, exactly half.

What this study does not tell you

It doesn't see who pitched, who was hurt, or who rested. A 1 and 9 stretch can mean a bad team or a good team that ran into three aces, and this test treats them the same. The last ten is a crude window; a different cutoff could read differently, though we saw no sign of a clean trend at any gap. The scoring table counts runs in the game, extra innings included, which is how a full game team total grades. And none of this says whether Thursday's prices are fair. It says the streak shouldn't be the reason you pay them.
