MLB Betting Guide: Totals, Pitching and Park Factors

Baseball is a totals and pitching market first. Here is how the price is built, which numbers move it, and where the recurring mistakes are.

No live data on this page. Sports Betting Prime does not publish live odds, projected statistics, consensus percentages or game predictions. Every figure below is either a definition, a worked example computed from the numbers shown, or carries a named source and the date it was current as of.

What actually moves this market

The starter is most of the number

A baseball line is anchored on the two probable starters. When a probable changes, the total and the moneyline both move before anything else has happened. Confirm the probable before you price anything.

Bullpen state is the second input

Innings thrown over the previous three days determine who is unavailable. A rested pen and a burned pen are worth a meaningful amount on a team total, and the information is public.

Park factors are real but already priced

Coors Field is the extreme case, and it is the one every model already accounts for. The exploitable version of park factor is the marginal one - roof state, wind direction, temperature - not the headline.

First five innings removes the bullpen

An F5 bet isolates the starters. If your read is entirely about the starting pitching matchup, F5 is the more honest expression of it than the full-game total.

Team totals isolate one lineup

If your view is "this offence is going to struggle" rather than "this team will lose", a team total says exactly that and avoids paying for the other side of the game.
