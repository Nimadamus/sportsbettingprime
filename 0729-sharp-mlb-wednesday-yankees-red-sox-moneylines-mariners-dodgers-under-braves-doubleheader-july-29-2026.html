Every Favorite On Tonight's Board Is Priced By Subtraction

July 29, 2026 | Moneyline and Total Market Read | Sports Betting Prime

Grant Holmes threw the first pitch of today's Mets-Braves doubleheader at 1:10 this afternoon in Queens, six hours later than either club actually wanted to play it. Tuesday night's game got washed out at Citi Field, and the makeup slid both teams into a split doubleheader that puts Chris Sale on the mound for game two anyway, on his normal turn, against a Mets team that did not ask for a longer day. That accident of weather is one of four plays on tonight's board, and it happens to fit a pattern running through the other three: every favorite tonight is priced short not because of who is hitting, but because of who is hurt, who just lost eight straight as a home favorite, or who has fifteen fewer wins than the team standing across the diamond.

Six and a half units are live across four games tonight: Yankees at White Sox, Red Sox at Athletics, Mariners at Dodgers, and the Sale-Scott nightcap between the Braves and Mets. Here is the full card, then the case for each one and the honest problem with it.

Yankees Moneyline: Down Three Regulars, Still The Play

New York carries a 61-46 record into Rate Field, a game and a half behind Tampa Bay in the AL East, and does it without Aaron Judge, who is on the 60-day injured list with a rib injury, without Giancarlo Stanton and Cody Bellinger, both on the 10-day list with leg issues, and without Carlos Rodon and Clarke Schmidt from the pitching staff. That is three regulars and two starters missing, and the moneyline is still minus 140. The reason sits on the mound: Cam Schlittler takes a 10-6 record, a 2.07 ERA and a 0.93 WHIP into a start against a White Sox lineup that has produced 157 strikeouts against him already this year. Chicago is a half game up on Cleveland in the AL Central at 55-51 and has its own injured list stretching well past Schlittler's counterpart Davis Martin, who is 9-5 with a 3.49 ERA. The Yankees have already won the first two games of this four-game set. A minus 140 price on a lineup this thin is a bet that Schlittler's individual gap over Martin is bigger than the gap created by three missing bats, not a bet that New York's offense is whole.

Three regulars on the injured list and the Yankees are still minus 140. That price is not a lineup argument. It is a bet that a 2.07 ERA against a 3.49 ERA is worth more than Judge, Stanton and Bellinger combined.
Red Sox Moneyline: A Road Team On A Heater Against A Home Team In Collapse

Boston has won 10 of its last 11 games away from Fenway, a stretch that puts the Red Sox at 55-51 overall and 30-22 specifically on the road heading into Sutter Health Park. The Athletics sit at 42-57, fourth in the AL West, and are 21-31 at what currently passes for home. The specific angle here is not the Red Sox roll, it is the building they are walking into: Oakland's Athletics have lost eight straight games as a home underdog immediately following a win, and they just beat Boston 4-3 in the previous game of this set. That is precisely the situation the trend describes. Patrick Sandoval starts for Boston with a 3.21 ERA across three appearances this season, a small sample with a 1.64 WHIP that is nothing to build a case around by itself. Jacob Lopez starts for Oakland with a 6.12 ERA overall and a specific 7.62 ERA in three home starts this year, an 0-3 mark that argues the split matters more than his season line. The honest read is that this is a park-and-trend case built on Lopez's home numbers and the after-a-win fade, not a case that Sandoval is pitching like an ace.

Mariners-Dodgers Under 9.5: The Total That Argues With Itself

Seattle sits at 53-55 and Los Angeles at 67-40, a 14-game gap in the standings between two teams sharing a mound tonight. Emerson Hancock starts for the Mariners with a 3.16 ERA and a 6-4 record, the best pitching line on tonight's board. Eric Lauer starts for the Dodgers with a 4.83 ERA and a 5-5 mark, and that number is the honest problem with betting under 9.5 tonight. A starter allowing better than four and a half earned runs a game is not, on his own numbers, an under pitcher. The case for the under rests on what sits behind him: a Dodgers bullpen and defense that has helped the club to 67 wins despite a starting rotation that is not universally strong top to bottom, and a Mariners offense that has scored enough to finish two games under .500 rather than well under it. This is the one play tonight built on a pitching staff's full depth rather than a single arm, and it is the one play where the honest counterargument, Lauer's own ERA, sits in plain sight on the same stat line used to justify the bet.

Braves Moneyline: Chris Sale Gets His Turn Regardless Of The Weather

Tuesday night's rain at Citi Field turned Wednesday into a split doubleheader, Sean Manaea against Grant Holmes at 1:10 this afternoon, then Chris Sale against Christian Scott at 7:10 tonight. Atlanta sits at 62-44, in playoff position, and New York sits at 45-62, seventeen games back and playing out a season that has already turned toward next year. Sale takes an 11-6 record and a 2.19 ERA into game two, the best ERA of any starter on tonight's board, against a Mets team throwing Christian Scott, who is 3-2 with a 3.13 ERA in a much smaller body of work. A rainout that forces a team to play two is usually treated as a scheduling inconvenience. For Atlanta tonight it is closer to a gift: Sale pitches on his normal rest regardless of the makeup date, against a last-place lineup that has to send its regulars back out for a second nine innings in the same afternoon.

Six And A Half Units, One Repeated Question

Line them up and the shape is consistent even though the sports and cities are not. Three moneylines and a total, and in three of the four cases the favorite is priced short because something is missing from the other side, an injured list, a home record in freefall, a last-place roster stretched across a doubleheader. Only the Dodgers total under leans on subtraction of a different kind, a bullpen and defense doing enough work that a 4.83 ERA starter does not sink the number by himself. None of that guarantees tonight goes the way the card is built. Schlittler still has to beat Martin, Sandoval still has to hold a lineup that just took a game off Boston, and Sale still has to pitch like the front of a rotation and not just look like one on paper. The prices above are where six and a half units are actually sitting tonight, and the reasoning behind each one is exactly as strong, or as thin, as it reads above.
