The July 2026 withdrawal

150 pages emptied

An audit found that the against-the-spread records, points-per-game figures, home records, defensive rankings, line-movement notes and sharp-action claims on the automatically generated sport pages were not sourced from any data provider. They were produced by a random number generator. Because the underlying numbers never existed, those pages could not be corrected, only emptied. The generator was deleted. The URLs were kept live rather than removed so that the record of what was published is not quietly erased.

Why consensus is paused

A counting bug, not a scraping bug

The scraper itself queries real picks and fails loudly rather than inventing data when a feed is unreachable. What was wrong was the presentation: a public-money percentage was bucketed into an integer and summed into the same counter as real handicapper picks, so a board reading “8x” could have meant “public money was above 75 percent” rather than “eight handicappers picked this”. Publishing stopped in July 2026 and has not resumed. The 60 archived boards remain readable and are not in the sitemap.

What this site does not do

It does not sell picks, publish a tipping record, or rank handicappers. It does not claim real-time pricing: there is no live odds feed attached to it, so any price on an archived page is the price that was available on the date that page names.

Historical pages

Every page that covered a specific date keeps its original URL, carries a banner naming that date and an archive breadcrumb, and has had its present-tense wording corrected. Pages are not deleted or redirected to tidy the archive.

A reference desk for how betting markets are priced. No page here sells a pick, ranks a handicapper or claims a record. Scores and schedules come from the ESPN public API. Consensus publishing is paused. Dated boards and withdrawn pages are kept at their original URLs in the archive, clearly labelled and out of the way.
