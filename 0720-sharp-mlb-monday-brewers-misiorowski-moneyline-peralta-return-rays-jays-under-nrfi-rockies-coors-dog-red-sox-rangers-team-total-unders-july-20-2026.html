Misiorowski Meets The Man He Replaced, And Six Of Eight Reads Point Under

July 20, 2026 | MLB Moneyline, Total, Team Total and NRFI Market Reads | Sports Betting Prime | Corrected 2026-07-21 | Corrected 2026-07-21

Monday night at American Family Field, Freddy Peralta walks back into the building where he spent his entire career through last season, wearing a New York Mets uniform, and the man who inherited his old rotation looks across the diamond at him. Jacob Misiorowski is 10-4 with a 1.62 ERA, a 0.76 WHIP, and a .148 opponent batting average. Peralta is 5-8 with a 4.66 ERA for a 42-58 Mets team that has fallen completely out of the race. The market put Milwaukee at minus 190, and the first instinct of every recreational bettor is the same: too expensive, never lay two dollars in baseball. That instinct is exactly why the number exists. This is the biggest gap between two starting pitchers on the entire July 20 board, and the market knows most people will refuse to pay for it.

That is the anchor of an eight-read Monday card totaling 14.5 units, and the theme running through the other seven plays is just as clear: the arms own this slate. Six of the eight reads point at unders or run suppression of some kind. Every record below comes straight from the MLB Stats API schedule feed for tonight, every pitching and team number was pulled from the live league data this session, and each read carries its honest counterpoint, because a card without one is a sales pitch, not analysis.

Mets At Brewers: The Reunion The Price Already Settled

Start with the story, then check it against the numbers, because this one survives both. Peralta gave Milwaukee a 2.70 ERA season in 2025, and the Brewers moved forward with the flame-throwing kid anyway. One year later the decision looks like organizational clairvoyance. Misiorowski leads this staff at 10-4 with a 1.62 ERA and a 0.76 WHIP, striking out 167 hitters in 111 innings while walking just 27. Opponents are hitting .148 against him. Peralta, meanwhile, has a 1.44 WHIP and a .257 average against for a Mets offense-starved roster, and the lineup behind him ranks near the bottom of the league at 4.09 runs per game with a .234 team average and 824 strikeouts. The 62-37 Brewers score 5.07 runs a game and hit .253 as a team. Twenty games separate these clubs in the win column.

The Brewers moneyline at minus 190 is the play at 3 units, the largest position on the card. The market read: minus 190 sounds heavy until you translate it, because it only implies about 65.5 percent, and a 62-37 team at home with the biggest starting pitching edge on the board clears that bar. The honest counterpoint is that price risk is real; a 3-unit loss at minus 190 sets the card back hard, Peralta knows this park and this organization intimately, and Misiorowski has been so good that any regression start hurts at this number. But betting is paying fair prices for real edges, and a .148 opponent average against a .234-hitting team is as real as an edge gets in July.

Milwaukee let a 2.70 ERA walk out the door because Misiorowski was ready. Monday the market asks whether you will pay minus 190 to side with that decision. The 0.76 WHIP says yes.
Rays At Blue Jays: Two Aces, One Short Total, And A First-Inning Lean

Rogers Centre gets the best combined pitching matchup of the night. Nick Martinez has quietly built one of the best seasons in the American League at 8-2 with a 2.65 ERA and a 1.12 WHIP across 105 and a third innings, walking just 19 batters all year. Dylan Cease answers with a 2.56 ERA, a .190 opponent average, and 148 strikeouts in 98 and a third innings, backed by only 5 home runs allowed, an absurd suppression number for 17 starts. The offenses do not scare either arm. Toronto sits at 46-53 scoring just 4.01 runs per game with a .242 average, and while the 56-42 Rays are a legitimate first-place club, their attack is contact-driven at 4.44 runs a game rather than slug-driven.

The under 7.5 at minus 115 is the play at 2 units, and the no run first inning at minus 134 rides with it at 1.5 units. The NRFI leg is not a vibes bet; it is a splits bet. Martinez has a 1.00 ERA in the first inning this season, holding hitters to a .185 average across 18 first frames. Cease has struck out 27 batters in his 17 first innings. The honest counterpoint belongs almost entirely to Cease's walks: 44 on the season and 10 of them in first innings, and free passes are how clean innings die. He is also facing the best team in the American League East. But when the worst outcome either starter has shown in the opening frame is traffic rather than damage, and the full-game matchup is a 2.65 ERA against a 2.56, both numbers are on the right side.

Athletics At Diamondbacks: Fading The Loudest ERA On The Board

The second-biggest position is the simplest read. Jeffrey Springs is 3-9 with a 6.08 ERA, and the number that matters most is 26, the home runs he has allowed in 97 and two-thirds innings. Every ballpark plays small when a starter serves that much contact in the air, and now he gets a Chase Field date with a 51-48 Arizona club that hits well enough at 4.37 runs per game to punish it. The catch, and it is a real one, is who Arizona sends out: Mitch Bratt, a rookie with exactly two big-league starts and six innings of career work. Betting minus 154 behind a six-inning resume requires acknowledging you are mostly betting against the other guy and on the Arizona bullpen behind him.

The Diamondbacks moneyline at minus 154 is the play at 2.5 units. The market read is that this line is about the 42-57 Athletics roster and Springs's 6.08 ERA, not about Bratt, and that is the correct way to price it. The Athletics have power at 122 home runs, which is precisely why Springs's profile is fatal in reverse: his 1.42 WHIP means constant traffic, and Arizona only needs a couple of loud swings to put this out of reach. If Bratt implodes early, minus 154 hurts. But one side of this game starts a pitcher who has allowed 26 homers, and the other side merely starts a question mark. Question marks beat known liabilities.

Nationals At Rockies: Plus Money At Altitude Against A Six-Start Rookie

Here is the one read on the card where the record screams no and the situation says yes. Colorado is 40-61, and Kyle Freeland's season line is ugly: 2-8 with a 7.36 ERA and a .319 average against. So why is the play the Rockies at plus 107? Because Washington's answer is Andrew Alvarez, a rookie left-hander with six major-league starts, a 1.45 WHIP, and 22 walks in just 49 innings, now asked to handle the most unforgiving pitching environment in baseball. Wildness at altitude is how three-run innings happen, and Colorado's offense is built for exactly this kind of night: 4.79 runs per game, a .255 team average, and 116 home runs, numbers that jump another level at home. The 50-50 Nationals do bring the loudest bats on the slate at 5.45 runs a game, which is why this is a coin-flip price and not a real favorite either way.

The Rockies moneyline at plus 107 is the play at 1.5 units. At plus money the bet only needs to win about 48.3 percent of the time, and this is a true toss-up: both starters are liabilities, both offenses can score, and the tiebreakers are the ballpark and which lineup has taken more at-bats in it. Freeland's 7.36 ERA is the entire counterpoint and it deserves respect; if this turns into a slugfest that stays close, the Washington bats are better. But you are being paid to take the home team in a stadium that ruins visiting rookies, against a starter whose command has not survived sea level yet. That is what plus 107 is for.

Twenty-two walks in 49 innings is a survivable flaw in Washington. At Coors Field it is a detonator. Plus 107 on the home team in a coin-flip game is the value side of the night.
Twins At Guardians: Joe Ryan And The Quietest Offense In The League

The smallest play on the card is the cleanest profile fit. Joe Ryan brings a 2.85 ERA, a 1.05 WHIP, and 128 strikeouts against just 25 walks to Progressive Field, where he meets a Cleveland lineup that has been the definition of quiet: 3.92 runs per game, a .230 team average, and 844 strikeouts for the 52-48 Guardians. Tanner Bibee's 3.90 ERA and 3-9 record undersell him a bit, since his 1.15 WHIP and .230 average against are the marks of a pitcher throwing better than his results, and the 49-51 Twins are a middle-of-the-pack offense that strikes out 817 times. The under 7.5 at minus 110 is the play at 1 unit. The counterpoint is Bibee's one true flaw, 20 home runs allowed, because solo shots are the fastest way to leak runs through an otherwise strong start, and Minnesota's 123 homers can exploit it. That flaw is why this is the one-unit leg instead of a two-unit leg. But a 2.85 ERA against a sub-four-runs-a-game offense is the kind of half of a game you can nearly bank, and it only takes one quiet side to keep 7.5 safe.

Two Team Totals: Capping Boston Against Baz, Capping Texas Against Fedde

The card closes with two team total unders at 4.5, and they share one logic: cap a roughly league-average offense against a starter plus bullpen that does not need to dominate, only to avoid disaster. In Boston, the 50-48 Red Sox score 4.21 runs per game with a .244 average and 802 strikeouts, and they get Shane Baz, whose 4.19 ERA comes with real swing-and-miss at 99 strikeouts. The Red Sox team total under 4.5 at minus 130 is the play at 1.5 units. Baz's 1.39 WHIP and 42 walks are the counterpoint, since traffic against Fenway's geometry is dangerous, but a team scoring 4.21 a game needs a full five to beat this number, and Payton Tolle's 3.11 ERA on the other side means Boston will likely be playing a tight, station-to-station game rather than piling on against garbage-time relievers.

In Arlington, the equation is even starker. The 50-49 Rangers have scored 4.16 runs per game, hitting .245 with 828 strikeouts, one of the most strikeout-prone offenses in the league relative to its output. Erick Fedde is no ace at 4.15 with a 1.42 WHIP across nine starts for the 52-46 White Sox, and that is the point: the Rangers team total under 4.5 at minus 140, 1.5 units, does not require Fedde to be good. It requires Texas to be Texas. And the game script helps, because Jacob deGrom, 7-5 with a 3.49 ERA, a 1.01 WHIP, and 122 strikeouts against 22 walks, gives Texas every reason to play a low-scoring, protect-the-lead game. When your own ace is dealing, nobody chases crooked numbers. The counterpoint is Fedde's traffic, walks and a .262 average against that can hand Texas cheap runs, and 16 homers off deGrom mean the game itself is not immune to loud innings. The bet is sized accordingly.

The Monday Card, Stacked

Eight reads, 14.5 units, one theme: pay for the arms, get paid by the bats that cannot answer them. The Brewers moneyline at minus 190 anchors everything at 3 units behind Jacob Misiorowski's 0.76 WHIP against the man he replaced. The Diamondbacks moneyline at minus 154 carries 2.5 units against Jeffrey Springs and his 26 home runs allowed. Rays-Blue Jays gets doubled up, the under 7.5 at minus 115 for 2 units and the NRFI at minus 134 for 1.5, because Nick Martinez and Dylan Cease are the two best pitchers sharing any mound tonight. The Rockies at plus 107 take Coors Field against a six-start rookie for 1.5 units, the Red Sox and Rangers team total unders at 4.5 cap two four-runs-a-game offenses for 1.5 units each, and the Twins-Guardians under 7.5 rounds it out at 1 unit behind Joe Ryan and the quietest lineup in the American League. The public will see minus 190 and flinch, see plus 107 on a 40-61 team and laugh. The card sees the same eight numbers and recognizes the prices are answers, not questions.
